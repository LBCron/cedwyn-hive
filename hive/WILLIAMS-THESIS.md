## The hypothesis (not a finding)

The Great House (Tŷ Mawr) was a manor house with inherent lordship over the Cedwyn estate lands (100+ acres, Leckwith / Llandough). The Williams family (possibly Vaughan descendants / original true freeholders) were manorial occupants and Welsh minor gentry from 1667. Mary Williams’s 1958 remark that hers was “a Welsh case of Crichel Down” may mean the whole Cedwyn estate was taken under compulsory / public purposes with a buy-back option that was never honoured, converting freeholders into tenants of Bute, who then divided the estate into parcels and systematically dispossessed them. Every parcel grievance (A, B, C, woodland, commons, playing fields, school land, 33 acres limeworks) is the missing buy-back. History was rewritten; the later 12-year permissive / registration pattern was the device that made Williams occupation appear permissive.

Parallel patterns noted for testing: Bute acquiring a parcel from a Williams of Caerphilly on death; Alfred Thomas d.1927 estate to Cardiff Corporation; Vaughan estate (no heirs) to Bute at auction (Ty Mawr reportedly outside that sale). Lambert Williams of Cardiff is a named lead to check.
